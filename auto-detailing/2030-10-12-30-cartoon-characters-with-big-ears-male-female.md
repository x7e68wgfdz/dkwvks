Why are there no good slang terms for ears? Eyes get “peepers,” your mouth is a “yap,” “trap,” or “kisser,” and a nose is a “schnoz.” Despite the lack of colloquialisms, ears are a popular way to communicate important information about a cartoon character. They can be expressive, played up for comedic effect, or used to signal that a character enjoys books and nerding out. Here, we’re going to look at some of the most notable character designs when it comes to ears for cartoon people, animals, and aliens/monsters. Famous Cartoon Characters with Big Ears

Big ears are typically played up for comedic value, added to create empathy with a character, or used to signal that a character is a bit nerdy. Some of the most iconic big-eared cartoon characters include:

People: Alfred E. Neuman, Mr. Mackey, Smitty, Nigel Thornberry

Animals: Dumbo, Bugs Bunny, Goofy, Despereaux Tilling

Aliens: Yoda, Stitch, Connie, Snarf, Momo, Dobby Steps Section 1 of 3: Humans Download Article 1

Alfred E. Neuman (Mad Magazine, 1955) The missing tooth, the freckles, the lazy eye, and the giant ears make Alfred E. Neuman the most iconic big-eared cartoon character of all time. The de facto mascot for Mad Magazine, he’s meant to represent the magazine’s carefree and rebellious attitude.[1]XResearch source 2

Mr. Mackey (South Park, 1997) It’s easy to miss the mumbling teacher’s giant ears because his head is so comically massive, but his ears are indeed massive. Even in the episode where he’s a child, he’s got massive lobes dangling from the sides of his head! Advertisement 3

Smitty (Dumbo, 1941) You probably don’t remember Smitty at a glance—he’s only in a few scenes of the film. Dumbo encounters him at the circus, where he’s hanging out with other bullies. They mock Dumbo’s big ears, which is especially ironic because Smitty’s ears happen to be profoundly large.

It’s actually an interesting character choice to have the kid with big ears bully the main character for having big ears. It makes you wonder if Smitty was bullied for his ears and is now projecting his insecurities onto others. 4

Nigel Thornberry (The Wild Thornberrys, 1998) Nigel and his giant ears make him the single most-widely represented character in wikiHow’s pantheon of funny characters. He ranked 6th for most iconic buck-teeth and 6th again for biggest noses. He’s even 3rd for our overall list of funniest-looking character designs. If we ever write an article on the top characters with goofy box-shaped torsos and adorable tiny arms, he’d probably win first place there, too. 5

Ichabod Crane (The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad, 1949)The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad was Disney’s adaptation of the story of Sleepy Hollow, where a headless horseman torments our poor protagonist. Crane’s character design is all about stretching the design to the extremes, and that’s visible in the size of his ears, as well. 6

Shaggy (Scooby-Doo, 1969) A potentially controversial selection, Shaggy’s ears may not seem particularly large at first glance. But his hair is doing a lot of work hiding the true size of those ears. Look at the shape of the ears based on what’s exposed. They have to be absolutely huge! 7

Vector (Despicable Me, 2010) Vector is a dweeb, and his ears look like swollen quarters. There. We said it. The antagonist from the Despicable Me series has ears big enough that he could probably use them as a parachute if he fell out of a plane. 8

Eustace (Courage the Cowardly Dog, 1999) It’s funny when a character with huge ears can’t hear very well. That’s the case with Eustace, one of Courage’s owners. Despite the massive ears, he frequently misunderstands people, which makes it even funnier when he starts screaming about Courage’s indiscretions. 9

Sheen (The Adventures of Jimmy Neutron, 2001) He’s neurotic, he’s obsessed with the superhero Ultra Lord, and he’s got ears that stick out the side of his head like spoilers on a Toyota. 10

Quasimodo (The Hunchback of Notre Dame, 1996) Quasimodo’s physical deformities are a core part of his character’s identity and motivation, so people don’t tend to notice his large ears. His character is a reminder to all of us that the size of the ears doesn’t matter—it’s what’s in your heart that counts! Advertisement Section 2 of 3: Animals Download Article 1

Dumbo (Dumbo, 1941) A character with ears so large they drive the central conflict of the film, Dumbo is a lovable young elephant with ears wide enough to help him take flight. Dumbo’s story is a reminder that the things people mock us for are often the things that make us special and unique, and if you happen to be an elephant with colossal ears, that often means the ability to take flight. 2

Bugs Bunny (The Looney Tunes, 1930) You may be thinking, “Well, yeah, he’s a rabbit…rabbits have long ears.” But Bugs’ ears are actually really long, even for a silly widdle wabbit. 3

Goofy (Mickey’s Revue, 1932) Goofy is an anthropomorphic dog of unclear breed origins, but even if he were one of the long-eared breeds (like a basset hound), his ears are still extremely long. We’re filing this one under the Bugs Bunny category of “human-like animals that have comically big ears even for their animal form.” 4

Despereaux Tilling (The Tale of Despereaux, 2008) Based on a 2003 children’s novel, The Tale of Despereaux follows the titular character, a mouse with colossal ears, on his quest to prevent a princess from being kidnapped. It’s all very charming and whimsical, which is why we’re not going to poke fun at Despereaux for looking like such a goober. 5

Lola Bunny (Space Jam, 1996) If Bugs makes the long ears look goofy, Lola makes them look alluring. Her character is proof that huge ears cannot hold you back if you’re destined for hot cartoon character greatness. 6

Droopy (Droopy Dog, 1943) These days we tend to make children’s cartoons that are full of joy, life, and vigor, but back in the day, we used to make children’s cartoons about stoic, manic-depressive basset hounds with severe emotional issues like a proper country!. 7

Mickey Mouse (Steamboat Willie, 1928) What makes Mickey’s ears so impressive is that they’re literally just shapes. He doesn’t need any ear lobes or ear canals or eardrums, just two big old black circles. This actually raises an important question now that we think about it…does Mickey use echolocation like a bat? He can’t hear with those huge black circles, can he? 8

Arthur (Arthur, 1996) We debated leaving Arthur alone because aardvarks do actually have really big ears IRL, but Arthur’s doing himself no favors with his fit. The giant glasses he rocks really draw attention towards the top of his head and make the massive ears more 9

Tom (Tom & Jerry, 1940) Honestly, they both have huge ears, but Jerry messes with Tom so much that we want to give him some love for once. For a cat, his ears are indeed huge, anyway. 10

Stimpy (Ren & Stimpy, 1991) We actually couldn’t remember what Ren and Stimpy are supposed to be, and we were kind of surprised upon looking it up. Stimpy is a Manx cat, and Ren (the big-eared naked mole rat thing) is a chihuahua, which is weird because chihuahuas typically have hair and very normal-sized ears. Yoda (Star Wars: Episode V: The Empire Strikes Back, 1980) There’s a lot notable about Yoda’s character design. He’s the size of a small dog, he’s green, he’s got a 6-pack on his forehead, and he talks like someone who is actively messing with you as a prank. So, it’s totally understandable if you didn’t catch the part where he has ridiculous wing-tipped ears. 2

Stitch (Lilo & Stitch, 2002) We really like Stitch’s ears. They’re expressive, which gives his character a lot of texture, and they’re set on the bottom half of his head, which is fascinating design for a cartoon alien. Two notches are also missing from his ears, which adds to his wild, untamable vibe. 3

Pikachu (Pokémon, 1997) There are a lot of Pokémon who have kind of silly ears (looking at you, Loudred). However, none of them are as culturally notable as Pikachu. The electric rat has very adorable, spear-shaped ears. They probably help him channel electricity or something. 4

Dopey (Snow White and the Seven Dwarves, 1937) It’s 100% possible that all of the dwarves have equally silly ears, but all of them wear hats that cover their ears—all of them except for Dopey. The mute and accident-prone dwarf is the slapstick comedic relief, and apparently “big ears” means “falls over a lot” or something. 5

Connie (Big Mouth, 2017) We don’t have much to say about Connie’s ears, because Connie is perfect in literally all ways and she can do no wrong. Just listen to the way she says “bubble bath.” 6

Snarf (Thundercats, 1985) We figured we’d put our least favorite big-eared alien thing right after our favorite. Snarf is comedic relief, but his obnoxious voice and downright creepy character design do more “haunting our dreams” than “making us laugh.” 7

Gizmo (Gremlins: Secrets of the Mogwai, 2022) We know what you’re thinking. “Gizmo isn’t a cartoon! The Gremlins were puppets!” Normally, you’d be right. But Gremlins: Secrets of the Mogwai is an animated TV show, and Gizmo is a core part of it, so he wins a slot! 8

Toothless (How to Train Your Dragon, 2010) This is a really fun one because the ears are a really important element of Toothless’s character design. Dragons don’t typically have ears, but the animators of How to Train Your Dragon gave Toothless these cat-like ears. The character can’t talk, but Toothless expresses himself regularly through body language, and those ears are a big part of it. Next time you rewatch the flick, watch the dragon’s ears! 9

Momo (Avatar: The Last Airbender, 2005) Aang’s loyal companion, Momo is an important part of the team, but he’s more important as a narrative device that occasionally makes you laugh. One of the recurring things we’ve learned in our research is that big-eared sidekicks are rarely meant to be taken seriously, and Momo is no different in this regard. 10
