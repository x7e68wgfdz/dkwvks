Pro Tip: When selecting seat foam, choose a long-wearing, high-density material at least 1 inch thick—as recommended in This Old House Magazine’s reupholstery guide. High-density foam holds its shape far longer than standard cushion foam, which can compress and bottom out within a year of regular use on a dining chair.

Choosing the Right Fabric to Reupholster a Dining Chair

Durability: Opt for fabrics designed for upholstery or home decor use.

Pattern: If using a patterned fabric, ensure you have enough material to center the design on each chair.

Color: Choose a color that complements your dining room decor.

The first step in reupholstering your dining chair is removing the existing fabric and padding. If the seat base is in good condition, you can reuse it. However, if it’s worn or damaged, you may need to create a new base.

Begin by unscrewing the seat base from the chair frame. Set the screws aside in a safe place, as you’ll need them to reattach the seat later.

Using an awl and pliers, remove the old fabric and staples from the seat base. Work slowly to avoid damaging the wood underneath. Create a New Seat Base (If Necessary)

If the existing seat base is beyond repair, you’ll need to create a new one. To create a new seat base, follow these steps.

Either trace the old base or measure the dimensions of the seat frame.

Subtract about 1/8 inch from each side to ensure a proper fit.

Use a jigsaw to cut the plywood to size. Add New Cushioning

Proper cushioning is necessary for comfort and the overall appearance of your reupholstered chair. Follow these steps to add new cushioning to your seat base.

For cushioning, use a high-density foam that’s at least 1 inch thick to match the dimensions of your seat base.

Trace the seat base onto the foam and cut it out.

Apply a fast-drying spray adhesive to both the foam and the seat base.

Press the foam onto the seat base, ensuring it’s centered and smooth.

For a rounded edge, spray adhesive on the foam’s edges, wait a few seconds until it’s tack, and fold the top edge over to stick to the seat base.

Cut a piece of 1/2-inch Dacron polyester batting slightly larger than the seat base to act as a buffer between the fabric and the foam.

Staple the batting to the center of each side of the seat base with the staple gun.

Work your way around the seat, pulling the batting taut as you staple. Apply the New Fabric

How To Reupholster a Dining Room Chair: Our Conclusion

Reupholstering a dining chair can improve your dining room’s appearance on a low budget. With the right tools, materials, and a bit of patience, you can transform old, worn chairs into stylish seating that looks almost brand new. This project not only saves money but also allows you to customize your chairs to perfectly match your home decor.

As noted in This Old House Magazine, if your chair’s existing seat board is worn or damaged, you can replace it with a piece of sturdy ¾-inch plywood cut with a jigsaw to fit the shape of your chair frame. Pair it with high-density seat foam at least 1 inch thick for a long-wearing cushion. Most wood-frame dining chairs can be easily unscrewed, replaced, and recovered, making this an accessible weekend project even for beginners.

Pro Tip: Amanda Brown, author of Spruce: A Step-by-Step Guide to Upholstery and Design, recommends assessing the bones of any chair before committing to reupholstery: “High-quality pieces are often masked beneath a layer of ratty, torn fabric. Carved wood, down cushions, and coil springs are good signs that you’ve found a gem in the rough.” She also warns to be leery of a wobbly frame, as repairs can get costly, and to steer clear of pieces that were stored outdoors since they could have mold or termite damage.
