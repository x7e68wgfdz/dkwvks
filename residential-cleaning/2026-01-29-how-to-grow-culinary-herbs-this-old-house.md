Select herbs based on your cooking preferences and growing conditions. Popular culinary herbs include basil, chives, dill, mint, oregano, parsley, rosemary, tarragon, and thyme.

Each has its own sunlight, water, and soil requirements, so figure out what each of your plants needs to grow ahead of time. For example, basil thrives in warm and sunny conditions with well-draining soil, while mint prefers partial shade and slightly moist soil. Container Gardening and Herbs

Container gardening is a great option for growing herbs, even if you have limited outdoor space. Follow these steps to set up a successful herb container garden:

Choose a container with enough drainage holes to prevent waterlogging. There should be 3–4 inches between each drainage hole.

Fill your container halfway with a mix of soil, compost, and perlite.

Plan the layout and avoid overcrowding. Know how much lateral space each herb needs—some herbs like 3 inches of free space, others require 3 feet. Mark where each plant will go.

Gently remove each plant from its starter pot and loosen the roots, knocking away old or excess soil. Plant them in their designated spots, deep enough to cover their roots completely.

Gently water your container garden. Double-check that any excess water drains out of the planter; if it doesn’t, check that the drainage holes are unobstructed and the soil isn’t too compacted.

Place your container garden in a spot that gets enough sunlight to keep your herbs happy.

Be sure to group herbs with similar growing requirements together. This will prevent one plant from out-competing another for resources. Adding a layer of mulch can also help retain moisture and regulate soil temperature.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, This Old House landscape contractor, warns: “Most people kill their herbs with too much water.” When planting in containers, he stresses that herbs need “very, very good drainage” and recommends a soil mix with compost and plenty of perlite. He also advises leaving about half an inch of space at the top of the pot rim so water has a little reservoir space to settle before flowing through. Indoor Herb Gardening

Growing herbs indoors allows for year-round access to fresh flavors. Follow these tips for successful indoor herb gardening:

Air circulation: Aerate your soil every few months to prevent fungal diseases.

Humidity: Increase humidity around your herbs by placing pots on trays filled with pebbles and water.

Light: Place herbs in a south-facing window for maximum natural light. If natural light is insufficient, supplement with grow lights.

Temperature: Most herbs prefer temperatures between 65 and 70 degrees Fahrenheit.

Indoor herb gardening also allows you to enjoy the greenery and fragrances of fresh herbs during colder months. Some herbs, like cilantro, thrive better indoors because of their sensitivity to extreme heat. Regularly rotate your pots for even growth and to prevent the plants from leaning toward the light source.

Pro Tip: One of the biggest challenges of growing herbs indoors is ensuring they get enough light. As This Old House contributor Carrie explains, some herbs need about eight hours of direct sunlight, which can be hard to achieve during winter months. Proper Care and Maintenance

To keep your herb garden happy and growing no matter its location, we recommend these tips:

Fertilizing: Use organic fertilizer sparingly to promote healthy growth without compromising flavor.

Watering: Most herbs prefer well-draining soil and moderate, consistent moisture. Avoid overwatering, which can lead to root rot.

Pruning: Regular pruning encourages bushier growth and prevents herbs from becoming leggy.

Sunlight: Provide adequate sunlight based on each herb’s requirements. Many culinary herbs need at least six hours of direct sunlight daily.

Closely monitor your herbs for any signs of stress, like yellowing leaves or drooping, and address any issues quickly. Harvesting and Using Your Herbs

Proper harvesting techniques help plants grow with maximum flavor. Here are some guidelines for harvesting common herbs:

Basil: Pinch off the top leaves to encourage bushier growth.

Chives: Trim leaves about 2 inches away from their base, like giving a haircut.

Parsley: Cut outer stems at the base, allowing new growth from the center.

Wait until your herbs reach about 6 inches high before harvesting. Even if you’re not using them for cooking, regular pruning promotes fuller growth.

Tip: For more robust aromas and flavors, harvest your herbs in the morning when their essential oils are most concentrated.

As the seasons change, you may want to move your herbs outdoors. Follow these steps for a smooth transition:

Gradually acclimate plants to outdoor conditions over 7–10 days.

Start by placing herbs in a shaded area for a few hours daily.

Slowly increase exposure to direct sunlight and outdoor temperatures.

Monitor plants closely for signs of stress during the transition.

Reverse the process when bringing herbs back indoors for the winter.

Gradually acclimate the plants to help them adjust to the new environment without shocking them. This type of transition can make or break a plant, so be patient and attentive throughout the process.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, This Old House landscape contractor, stresses the importance of timing: “You need to know the date of the last frost in your region because if we put these outside and there was a frost, the chances are they’d all be killed. So, obviously, go beyond the last frost.” Common Challenges and Solutions

Even those with experienced green thumbs can encounter issues when growing herbs. Here are some common problems and solutions:

Bolting: Prevent herbs from flowering prematurely by watering and harvesting regularly.

Leggy growth: Increase light exposure and prune regularly to promote bushier growth.

Pest infestations: Use natural pest control methods, such as neem oil or insecticidal soap.

Yellowing leaves: This may indicate overwatering or nutrient deficiency. Adjust watering habits and consider fertilizing. Seasonal Care

Different seasons require different care approaches for your herbs. During the summer, be sure they receive enough water—especially during heatwaves. Shield them from excessive afternoon sun to prevent scorching.
