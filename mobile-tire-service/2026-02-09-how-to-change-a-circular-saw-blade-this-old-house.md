Changing the blade on your circular saw is an essential skill for any DIY enthusiast or professional carpenter. An appropriately sharp blade ensures clean cuts and safe operation of your tool. This guide will walk you through the process of changing a circular saw blade, from safety precautions to proper installation techniques. This Old House general contractor Tom Silva demonstrates the correct and safe way to install a new blade on a portable circular saw.

Safety First: Preparing To Change the Circular Saw Blade

Before you begin the process of changing your circular saw blade, prioritize safety. Taking the proper precautions will help prevent accidents and ensure a smooth blade replacement process.

Pro Tip: Tom Silva, This Old House general contractor, starts every blade change the same way: “The first thing we wanna start with is unplug the power cord from the wall or remove the battery from the saw.” It’s a simple step, but it’s the most critical one—you never want that blade turning while you’re working on it. Disconnecting the Power Source

The first and most important step in changing a circular saw blade is to disconnect the power source. This prevents accidental activation of the saw during the blade change process.

For corded saws: Unplug the saw’s power cord from the electrical outlet or extension cord.

For cordless saws: Remove the battery from the saw.

Double-check that the saw is completely powered off before proceeding. Gathering Necessary Tools

Before you start, make sure you have all the required tools on hand. This will make the process more efficient and reduce the risk of injury from improvising with inappropriate tools.

Blade wrench (usually provided with the saw)

Clean cloth (for wiping down the arbor and blade) New circular saw blade

Work gloves (optional but recommended for added safety) Selecting the Right Blade

Choosing the right replacement blade optimizes performance and safety. Consider the following factors when selecting a new blade:

Arbor size: The center hole of the blade must fit the saw’s arbor perfectly.

Blade diameter: Ensure the new blade matches the size of your saw (commonly 7-1/4 inches for portable circular saws).

Maximum RPM rating: The new blade should be rated for at least the maximum speed of your saw in revolutions per minute (RPM).

Tooth count and type: Choose based on the material you’ll be cutting (e.g., choose a blade with more teeth for making finer cuts in hardwood).

Pro Tip: This Old House’s Tom Silva gauges blade condition by inspecting the teeth closely: “I gauge sharpness by the look of the teeth—sharp points and crisp edges.” He recommends checking midblade, where most of the cutting happens, and discarding a blade the moment you suspect it’s dull. Removing the Old Saw Blade

With safety measures in place and tools ready, you can now remove the old blade from your circular saw. Locate the Arbor Lock

Most modern circular saws feature an arbor lock mechanism that simplifies blade removal and installation.

Look for a button or switch near the blade housing. Press and hold the arbor lock button.

Rotate the blade slowly until you feel the lock engage, which will prevent the arbor from spinning. Loosen the Arbor Nut

Before installing the new blade, take a moment to inspect it and ensure it’s ready for use.

Special Considerations When Changing Circular Saw Blades

While the basic process of changing a circular saw blade is straightforward, certain types of saws and blades require special considerations. Worm Drive Saws

Worm drive saws often have a different blade mounting system compared to sidewinder circular saws. Some blades come with a diamond-shaped knockout for use with worm drive saws.

If your saw has a diamond-shaped arbor, you may need to remove the diamond insert from the center of the new blade. To do so, place the blade over two wood blocks for support. Use a hammer and punch to carefully tap out the diamond insert. This allows the blade to fit properly on the diamond-shaped arbor of the worm drive saw. Left-Blade vs. Right-Blade Saws

The direction in which you turn the wrench to loosen or tighten the arbor nut can vary depending on whether your saw has the blade on the left or right side of the motor.

For saws with the blade on the right of the motor, turn the wrench to the right to loosen. For saws with the blade on the left of the motor, turn the wrench to the left to loosen. Always turn the wrench in the direction that the blade cuts when loosening the nut. Blade Tightness

Tighten the arbor nut securely, but avoid using excessive force. Over-tightening can make it extremely difficult to remove the blade in the future. A properly tightened blade shouldn’t slip during use but should still be removable with reasonable effort when needed.

Additional Tips and Tricks for Circular Saw Blades

Here are some extra tips to ensure a successful blade change and prolong the lifespan of your saw and its blades. Blade Compatibility

While most circular saw blades are designed to be versatile, always double-check compatibility with your specific saw model. Some saws may have unique arbor sizes or blade guard designs that require specific blades. Maintaining Blade Sharpness

Regularly inspect your blade for dullness or damage. Cutting with a dull blade can lead to splintering, inaccurate cuts, and motor strain. Investing in a blade sharpener or knowing where to get blades professionally sharpened can extend the life of your blades. Using a Blade with a Kerf

The kerf of the blade is the width of the cut it makes. Using a blade with the appropriate kerf thickness for your saw can lead to smoother cuts and reduced wear on the motor. Thicker kerf blades are suitable for heavy-duty cutting, while thinner kerf blades are ideal for precise, fine cuts. Avoiding Blade Warp

Store your blades flat in a dry area to prevent warping. Warped blades can cause wobbling and inaccurate cuts. Using blade protectors or original packaging can help maintain the integrity of the blade. Circular Saw Blade Maintenance and Care

Regular maintenance of your circular saw and its blades can extend their lifespan and ensure optimal performance. Cleaning the Saw

After changing the blade, take a moment to clean your saw. Use a brush or compressed air to remove sawdust and debris from the blade housing. Wipe down the arbor and flanges with a clean cloth. Check that the blade guard moves freely and returns to its closed position. Blade Storage

Proper storage of your circular saw blades can help maintain their sharpness and prevent damage. Keep blades separated to avoid teeth contacting each other. Store blades in a dry place to prevent rust, and use blade guards or cases to protect the teeth from damage. Regular Inspection
