High elf fantasy names are beautiful, elegant, and sometimes even a bit alien-sounding. That's partly because most of the Elven names people are familiar with come from a fictional language created by J.R.R. Tolkien (author of The Lord of the Rings). However, some names from real-world languages like Welsh and Celtic also sound so graceful and other-worldly that they could easily fit an elf! If you're interested in building an elf character for Dungeons and Dragons, creating characters for a story, or even naming your baby, we've got you covered. Keep reading for male, female, and gender-neutral Elven names straight out of a world of mist and starlight.

Pick a name with an element or an aesthetic that matches your type of elf. The elf types have distinct moods, mannerisms, and visual aesthetics, and their names should reflect these differences. For example, a high elf is associated with royalty, beauty, and elegance, whereas a dark elf is associated with shadows, grit, and hard edges. Here are some guidelines to help you come up with a name for your elf character based on which type you've chosen:

Choose a long and regal-sounding name for a high elf, like Celebrimbor Lumina or Thalassiel Astra.

Pick a name with a nature-inspired meaning for a wood elf, like Nerissa Stargazer.

Create a name with dark, shadowy connotations for a dark elf, like Nyx Shadowmere or Morvandir Gloomwood.
