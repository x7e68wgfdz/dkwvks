Whether you use an external Wi-Fi adapter or are trying to optimize your computer’s built-in network adapters, it can be helpful to adjust your adapter’s settings. In this article, we’ll go over a couple of ways to access and find, open, and change adapter options in Windows 10 and 11. You can access some settings in Device Manager and others in Control Panel. Changing Adapter Settings in Windows

To change adapter settings in Device Manager, look up what brand of adapter you have, then find it in the Network Adapters. Right-click it and select Properties.

Once you find your adapter, you can tweak the settings to get the best performance out of it.

To change connection type priority or set how your adapter obtains its IP address, open the Change Adapter Settings menu in Control Panel. Steps Section 1 of 4: Ways to Access Adapter Options Download Article 1

To adjust your settings to get the best performance out of your adapter, continue to the next section. Advertisement Section 3 of 4: Adapter Options to Boost Internet Speed Download Article 1

Update your adapter’s settings to fix a slow internet connection. Before you start messing around with your adapter’s advanced settings, try some basic troubleshooting steps to get your connection working properly again. If that doesn’t work, you can try applying the following settings in the menu you accessed through the Device Manager. Keep in mind that not all of these might be available depending on your adapter model:[7]XResearch source

802.11n Channel Width (2.4 GHz & 5 GHz): Set this value to Auto so your adapter can automatically connect to the best available Wi-Fi band.

[Adapter Options Windows](https://adapter-options-windows.themaplelane.com/adapter-options-windows/20260826.html)

802.11n / 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) Mode: Set this value to Enabled unless you regularly experience dropped connections with a newer router, in which case Disabled may work better.

Preferred Band: Select Prefer 5 GHz or 5G First. Your adapter will prioritize connecting to faster 5 GHz router bands unless the 2.4 GHz connection is stronger. (This only works if your router uses the same SSID for both bands.)

Wireless Mode: Set this value to the highest protocol available in the dropdown menu.

Roaming Aggressiveness: For a more stable connection, choose the lowest option for computers you only use in one location. If you tend to physically move around with your computer, select the Medium option.

Transmit Power: Choose the highest available option (or 5). This will set your adapter to transmit the strongest possible signal to your router.

Throughput Booster/Enhancement:Disable this option unless your computer is the only one on the network. Otherwise, it will prevent other devices on the network from making a strong connection to your Wi-Fi.

Fat Channel Intolerant: Set this option to Disabled to boost your transfer speeds. 2

Save your changes and restart. Once you’ve made the desired changes, click OK and close the Device Manager. Then, reboot your computer to make sure the changes fully take effect. Advertisement Section 4 of 4:

Changing Adapter Settings in Control Panel Download Article 1

Open the Network and Sharing Center. The Network and Sharing Center in your Control Panel allows you to access a different set of adapter settings from the ones in Device Manager. To get there in Windows 10 or 11:

Search for Control Panel in the Start menu and open Control Panel from the results.

Go to Network and Internet > Network and Sharing Center (or just select Network and Sharing Center if the Large icons view is selected). 2

Click Change Adapter Settings. It’s in the left menu bar. A window showing your various network adapters will open. 3

Double-click an adapter to open its settings. Select the adapter for which you want to change settings. Double-click it to open a new window. From there, you can:

Click Enable or Disable to turn the adapter on or off.

Click Diagnose to troubleshoot connection issues.

Click Properties to access additional settings. You can also right-click the adapter and select Properties to go straight to this menu. 4

Change adapter priority in the Properties menu. If you have multiple network adapters active (e.g., for Ethernet vs. Wi-Fi), you can use this menu to choose what type of connection your PC prioritizes when multiple options are available. To do so, click Properties at the bottom of your adapter’s menu. Then:[8]XResearch source

Select Internet Protocol Version 4 (TCP/IPv4) and click Properties.

Click Advanced at the bottom of the window. Uncheck the “Automatic Metric” option.

[Adapter Options Windows](https://adapter-options-windows.curblist.xyz/adapter-options-windows/20260826.html)

Enter a number in the Interface Metric text field.

The lower the number, the higher the priority, so give lower numbers to connections you want to prioritize and higher numbers to ones you don’t want to prioritize.

For example, if you want to prioritize your Ethernet connection over Wi-Fi, you could enter a 1 in the Interface Metric field for your Ethernet adapter, and a 10 for your Wi-Fi adapter. Click OK when you’re done. 5

Change how your adapter obtains an IP or DNS address. If you need to use a specific IP address or DNS server, you can also do so from the Properties menu.

In the main Properties menu for your adapter, select Internet Protocol Version 4 (TCP/IPv4) and click Properties.

In the window that opens, select whether you want to obtain your IP address and DNS server address automatically or use a specific address.
